# Australian Health Performance Framework (AHPF)

## Overview

**Main goal:**
Improve health outcomes and ensure the sustainability of the Australian health system.

**Key components:**

- Health System Conceptual Framework
- Health System Performance Logic Model

**Five key domains:**

1. Determinants of health and wellbeing
2. Health system
3. Health status
4. Health system context
5. Equity

**Equity monitoring:**
Equity is monitored through the disaggregation of performance measure data for different populations and sub-groups.

## Framework Background

The **Australian Health Performance Framework (AHPF)** was endorsed by the Australian Health Ministers’ Advisory Council in September 2017.

It brings together the former:

- National Health Performance Framework, and
- Performance and Accountability Framework

to provide a single, authoritative vehicle for presenting information about the performance of Australia’s health system. The AHPF is intended to have primacy over other performance frameworks within the health sector.

The overarching objective of the AHPF is to facilitate improved health outcomes for all Australians and to ensure the long-term sustainability of the Australian health system.

The framework builds on Australian and international experience with performance and outcomes frameworks. It enables:

- effective categorisation of health system components,
- understanding of their inter-relationships and scope, and
- dynamic measurement of performance across the system.

The AHPF comprises two core elements:

- a **Health System Conceptual Framework**, and
- a **Health System Performance Logic Model**.

## Health System Conceptual Framework

The conceptual framework is structured around **five key domains**.

---

### 1. Determinants of Health and Wellbeing

This domain considers factors that influence the health status and healthcare needs of Australians. Many of these factors may sit outside the traditional boundaries of the health system.

Reporting on health determinants highlights both:

- the demand for health system services, and
- the need for multi-sectoral approaches to improve health outcomes.

Key determinants include:

- socioeconomic factors
- health behaviours
- personal biomedical factors
- environmental factors

---

### 2. Health System

This domain captures the activities and qualities of the health care system across all sectors, settings and organisational levels.

It highlights the requirement for care delivery to be:

- safe
- accessible
- high quality

Measures may be viewed from both patient and provider perspectives and may capture:

- activity levels
- outputs
- outcomes of care

Key dimensions include:

- effectiveness
- safety
- appropriateness
- continuity of care
- accessibility
- efficiency
- sustainability

---

### 3. Health Status

The health status domain represents the outcomes of all factors that shape health.

It includes:

- impairments, disabilities and handicaps resulting from disease
- incidence and prevalence of conditions across the community

Health status is reflected for individuals, cohorts and populations in terms of:

- health condition
- human function
- wellbeing
- death

---

### 4. Health System Context

This domain reflects the broader contextual factors that influence health system performance, including:

- demographics
- community and social capital
- governance and structure
- financing
- workforce
- infrastructure

It also encompasses:

- information systems
- research
- evidence to inform decision-making

These contextual issues are essential for informed planning and for sustaining the health system over time.

---

### 5. Equity

Equity underpins all domains of the framework, reflecting the objective of improving health outcomes for **all** Australians.

The AHPF explicitly recognises the need to monitor equity across:

- determinants of health
- the health system
- health status

for different populations and sub-groups.

Equity monitoring is achieved through appropriate disaggregation of performance measure data, which may include:

- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people
- people living in different geographic areas
- different socioeconomic groups
- other population groups relevant to the measure

Within the AHPF, **equity** is defined as the minimisation of avoidable differences between individuals or groups.

In applying equity to specific indicators:

- some indicators aim for equal treatment of individuals or groups in similar circumstances, while
- others require differential treatment based on differing levels of need.
