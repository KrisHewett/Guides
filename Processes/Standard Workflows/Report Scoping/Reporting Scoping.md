# Reporting Scoping

Reporting Parameters for Request Scoping

> Completing every question is *not* the goal. Surfacing uncertainty, disagreement, or weak assumptions early is more valuable than completeness.  
> A successful outcome may include clearly articulated uncertainties that remain unresolved.
>
> Where known, definitions and references should be explicit rather than assumed.  
> Including version, date, and source (with links or files where available) helps reduce ambiguity.

**Legend:** ⚠️ = **Decision‑Critical** question.

---

## 1. Purpose & Decision Context

Clarifies why the report exists and how its outputs are expected to be relied upon.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ What is the primary purpose of this report? | *What this report is intended to contribute to understanding, oversight, or assurance — and what it is not intended to resolve.* |
| ⚠️ What decision(s) will this report inform? | *Which decisions might reasonably be influenced by this output, and which decisions should remain outside its scope.* |
| ⚠️ What is the consequence if this report is wrong or misleading? | *The kinds of impact that could arise if findings are over‑trusted, misread, or taken out of context.* |
| Is this report exploratory or formal? | *Whether the output is expected to support learning and discussion, or to be relied upon more definitively.* |

---

## 2. Scope Definition

Describes the intended boundaries of the report, including what is excluded.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What subject matter is in scope? | *The specific areas, issues, or activities this report is intended to focus on, recognising that other issues may exist.* |
| What is explicitly out of scope? | *Areas or questions that this report should not be used to address, even if readers are tempted to infer them.* |
| What population or cohort applies? | *Who is represented in the data, and who is not, based on inclusion or exclusion choices.* |
| What timeframe applies? | *The period covered and how that period limits what change or stability can be inferred.* |
| What level of detail is required? | *The degree of aggregation that is appropriate, given privacy, stability, and intended use.* |

---

## 3. Stakeholders & Use Context

Highlights how different audiences may interpret or rely on the same output.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ Who is the **Decision‑maker**? | *Who is most likely to act on the report, and whose judgement the output is primarily intended to support.* |
| ⚠️ Who is the **Assurance‑seeker**? | *Who may rely on the report for confidence or oversight, rather than direct action.* |
| ⚠️ Who is the **Risk‑holder**? | *Where the consequences sit if the report is misunderstood or used inappropriately.* |
| Are there secondary audiences? | *Who else may read or reuse the output, even if it was not designed primarily for them.* |

---

## 4. Data Sources & Inputs

Summarises what is currently known about the data informing the report.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What are the primary data sources? | *Which systems or collections the report will draw from, and what those sources inherently do or do not capture.* |
| Are secondary or reference datasets used? | *Any contextual data that may frame interpretation, and the limits of its comparability.* |
| How current is the data? | *How recent the information is, and how that constrains statements about current conditions.* |
| Are there known data quality issues? | *Any known gaps, delays, or inconsistencies that may limit confidence in precision.* |
| Will datasets be linked? | *Whether combining sources introduces additional assumptions or ambiguity.* |

---

## 5. Definitions & Business Rules

Records how key terms, measures, and rules are understood for this request.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ How are key terms defined? | *How important terms are being interpreted for this report, particularly where meanings may vary.* |
| How are metrics calculated? | *How measures are derived, and how that method may differ from other reports or expectations.* |
| What inclusion/exclusion rules apply? | *Which records contribute to results, and how that shapes what the report represents.* |
| How is missing data handled? | *How gaps in data are treated, and how that treatment may influence interpretation.* |
| Which definition version applies? | *Which versioned definitions or policies are being relied upon, where relevant.* |

---

## 6. Analytical Expectations

Indicates the intended analytical approach and level of depth.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What analysis type is intended? | *Whether the analysis is limited to description, or extends to comparison or pattern exploration.* |
| Is statistical testing expected? | *Whether results are expected to indicate difference or change, or simply describe observed values.* |
| What stratifications are required? | *Which breakdowns are meaningful, and where small numbers may limit stability.* |
| Are comparators required? | *Any reference points being used, and the assumptions that make those comparisons reasonable or risky.* |
| What assumptions underpin the analysis? | *Assumptions that are being accepted for practicality, rather than because they can be verified.* |

---

## 7. Reporting Preferences (Outputs & Format)

Captures preferences for presentation, delivery, and frequency.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What output format is required? | *How presentation choices may influence perception of patterns or differences.* |
| How much interpretation is expected? | *The balance between presenting data and offering commentary, recognising reliance risk.* |
| How often is the report produced? | *Whether the output is a one‑off snapshot or part of an ongoing series, and how that affects interpretation.* |
| How will it be distributed? | *Who will have access to the output and how that context may shape reading.* |
| Are accessibility needs required? | *Any requirements that affect how clearly or consistently information can be interpreted.* |

---

## 8. Timing & Dependencies

Notes timing expectations and factors that may affect delivery or use.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a fixed deadline? | *How time constraints may limit validation, completeness, or refinement.* |
| What dependencies exist? | *External factors that may affect availability, quality, or timing of inputs.* |
| What happens if timing slips? | *How delays would affect usefulness or decision‑making.* |
| Is refresh or update expected? | *Whether outputs should be treated as provisional or stable.* |

---

## 9. Privacy, Security & Sensitivity

Identifies privacy or sensitivity considerations affecting handling or interpretation.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the report include personal data? | *Whether identifiability constrains sharing or level of detail.* |
| Are sensitive attributes included? | *Which aspects of the data require more cautious interpretation or handling.* |
| Who can access the output? | *The boundaries around who may rely on or reuse the report.* |
| Is there small‑number risk? | *Where low counts may limit robustness or increase disclosure risk.* |
| How long is the output retained? | *How long the report may be reused beyond its original context.* |

---

## 10. Assumptions, Limitations & Caveats

Surfaces uncertainty, constraints, and limits on interpretation.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ What are the known limitations of this data or analysis? | *Where confidence should be reduced and conclusions held lightly.* |
| What assumptions are being made that cannot be verified? | *Assumptions accepted for practicality rather than evidence.* |
| What behaviours influence whether this data exists at all? | *How reporting, incentives, or culture shape what is observed.* |
| Should any decisions or interpretations remain intentionally unresolved at delivery? | *Which questions should remain open to avoid over‑reach.* |
| ⚠️ Which conclusions or uses of this report should be explicitly prohibited? | *How the report should not be used, even if the data appears to support it.* |
| How is uncertainty communicated to the reader? | *How limits and caveats are made visible without overstating precision.* |
| What related questions are out of scope but likely to be inferred? | *Likely inferences that readers may draw despite being unsupported.* |

---

## 11. Change Control & Re‑scoping

Provides visibility over how shifts in scope or intent are recognised.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What constitutes a scope change? | *What would materially alter the intent or meaning of the report.* |
| How are changes surfaced? | *How shifts are made visible rather than absorbed silently.* |
| How are versions managed? | *How drafts and relied‑upon outputs are distinguished.* |
| What question might this report start answering if misused? | *How the output could be stretched beyond its intended purpose.* |

---

## 12. Success Criteria

Articulates what “fit for purpose” means in this context.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What does success look like? | *Whether the report supports its intended use without encouraging over‑interpretation.* |
| What is the minimum acceptable output? | *What is sufficient without implying certainty that is not warranted.* |
| ⚠️ What misinterpretation would we most want to prevent? | *Which misunderstanding would carry the greatest risk if it occurred.* |
| What would make this unusable? | *Conditions under which the output would no longer be trusted or relied upon.* |
