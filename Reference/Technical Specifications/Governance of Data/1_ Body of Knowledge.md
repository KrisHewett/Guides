# Data Management Body of Knowledge (DAMA‑DMBOK)  

---

## 1. Overview

The **Data Management Body of Knowledge (DAMA‑DMBOK)** is a globally recognised **reference guide** produced by **DAMA International** that describes the **discipline of data management**.

It provides:
- A **common language** for data management
- A structured view of **data management functions**
- Conceptual guidance on **roles, activities, and interactions**

It does **not** prescribe how data management must be implemented in any specific organisation.

---

## 2. Purpose of DAMA‑DMBOK

DAMA‑DMBOK exists to:
- Define the **scope of data management as a profession**
- Reduce ambiguity by establishing **shared terminology**
- Help organisations **understand what capabilities exist**
- Support education, capability planning, and discussion

It is intended to answer:
> “What does data management include, and how do its parts relate?”

---

## 3. What DAMA‑DMBOK Is Not

To avoid misuse, it is important to state explicitly that DAMA‑DMBOK is:

- **Not a standard**
- **Not certifiable**
- **Not a compliance framework**
- **Not a maturity model**
- **Not a governance operating model**
- **Not a technical architecture or tool specification**

Claims of “DMBOK compliance” are therefore **meaningless** without context.

---

## 4. Core Conceptual Structure

DAMA‑DMBOK is organised into **knowledge areas**, each representing a major domain of data management activity.

At the centre is **Data Governance**, which provides coordination and decision‑making alignment across all other areas.

### Central Function
- **Data Governance**  
  Establishes decision rights, accountability, policies, and oversight for data.

### Supporting Knowledge Areas

1. **Data Architecture**  
   Alignment of data structures with business and system architecture.

2. **Data Modelling and Design**  
   Conceptual, logical, and physical data models.

3. **Data Storage and Operations**  
   Databases, platforms, and operational data handling.

4. **Data Security**  
   Protection of data confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

5. **Data Integration and Interoperability**  
   Movement and synchronisation of data across systems.

6. **Document and Content Management**  
   Management of unstructured and semi‑structured information.

7. **Reference and Master Data Management**  
   Shared, authoritative data such as people, locations, or organisations.

8. **Data Warehousing and Business Intelligence**  
   Reporting, analytics, and decision‑support data environments.

9. **Metadata Management**  
   Definitions, lineage, context, and meaning of data.

10. **Data Quality Management**  
    Measurement, monitoring, and improvement of data fitness for purpose.

Each knowledge area describes:
- Objectives
- Typical activities
- Roles involved
- Inputs and outputs
- Relationships to other areas

---

## 5. Role of Data Governance in DAMA‑DMBOK

Within DAMA‑DMBOK:
- Data governance **coordinates** rather than executes
- It establishes **who decides**, not how tasks are performed
- It relies on other knowledge areas to deliver outcomes

A common misinterpretation is treating governance as a substitute for:
- Data quality management
- Metadata management
- Architecture and integration

DAMA‑DMBOK explicitly treats governance as **enabling**, not replacing, these functions.

---

## 6. Intended Organisational Use

Appropriate uses of DAMA‑DMBOK include:
- Designing or reviewing **data management capability maps**
- Creating **data governance charters** and role descriptions
- Identifying gaps or overlaps in data‑related activities
- Supporting education and onboarding of data professionals
- Framing discussions between business, analytics, and IT

Inappropriate uses include:
- Using knowledge areas as mandatory checklists
- Copying role definitions without contextual authority
- Treating the model as an implementation blueprint

---

## 7. Relationship to Other Frameworks and Standards

DAMA‑DMBOK is **complementary** to other governance and management instruments:

- **ISO/IEC 38505** – Data governance principles and executive oversight  
- **ISO/IEC 38500** – Governance of IT  
- **ISO/IEC 27001** – Information security management systems  
- **COBIT** – Control and assurance orientation  
- **Sector legislation and policy** – Mandatory requirements (e.g. health, privacy)

A common pattern:
- Governance standards explain **why and who**
- DAMA‑DMBOK explains **what exists**
- Local policy and systems define **how it is done**

---

## 8. Practical Implications for Reporting and Analytics

In reporting‑heavy environments (e.g. safety, performance, clinical analytics), DAMA‑DMBOK implies the organisation should be able to explain:

- Where data originates (architecture and integration)
- What the data means (metadata and definitions)
- Whether the data is reliable (data quality)
- Who is accountable (governance and stewardship)
- How data is protected and accessed (security)

It does **not** define:
- KPIs or indicators
- Reporting frequency
- Acceptable thresholds
- Tooling or platforms

---

## 9. Limitations and Risks

**Limitations**
- High‑level and intentionally generic
- Broad coverage without prioritisation
- No enforcement or validation mechanism

**Common risks**
- Over‑engineering data governance structures
- Role proliferation without decision authority
- Treating documentation as evidence of capability
- Confusing completeness with effectiveness

---

## 10. Positioning Statement

DAMA‑DMBOK should be understood as:

> A **map of the data management discipline**, not directions for a specific organisation.

Its value lies in **shared understanding**, not prescription.
