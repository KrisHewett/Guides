# ISO/IEC 38505 - Governance of Data

---

## 1. What ISO/IEC 38505 Is

**ISO/IEC 38505** is an international standard that extends **ISO/IEC 38500 (Governance of IT)** to address the **governance of data as an organisational asset**.

It does **not** define technical controls, architectures, or data management processes.  
Instead, it sets out **principles and governance questions** for those who direct and control organisations.

In practical terms, it answers:
> “What should governing bodies *expect, require, and oversee* in relation to data?”

---

## 2. What ISO/IEC 38505 Is Not

To avoid misuse, it is important to be explicit:

- It is **not** a compliance checklist  
- It is **not** a data management framework (e.g. DAMA-DMBOK)  
- It is **not** a security standard (e.g. ISO/IEC 27001)  
- It does **not** prescribe tooling, roles, or operating models  

Its role is **governance alignment**, not operational design.

---

## 3. Intended Use

ISO/IEC 38505 is intended to be used to:

- Guide **boards and executives** in setting expectations for data governance
- Support **decision-making and oversight** about data-related risk and value
- Align data practices with **organisational objectives, ethics, and obligations**
- Provide a **common governance language** across business, clinical, and technical domains

Typical use cases include:
- Framing data governance charters
- Informing executive dashboards and assurance reporting
- Assessing gaps in accountability and decision rights
- Anchoring data governance discussions to recognised principles

---

## 4. Structure of the Standard

ISO/IEC 38505 follows the **Evaluate - Direct - Monitor (EDM)** governance model used in ISO/IEC 38500.

### 4.1 Evaluate
Governing bodies should evaluate:
- Whether data is being used **ethically, lawfully, and appropriately**
- Whether data supports organisational strategy and outcomes
- Whether risks associated with data are understood and acceptable

### 4.2 Direct
Governing bodies should direct:
- Clear **accountability** for data ownership and stewardship
- Policies for data quality, access, protection, retention, and use
- Alignment between data initiatives and organisational priorities

### 4.3 Monitor
Governing bodies should monitor:
- Performance against agreed data objectives
- Compliance with laws, policy, and ethical expectations
- Emerging risks, incidents, and systemic weaknesses

---

## 5. Core Governance Principles (Applied to Data)

ISO/IEC 38505 applies the ISO/IEC 38500 principles to data governance. Interpreted for data, these include:

1. **Responsibility**  
   Data accountabilities are defined and understood.

2. **Strategy**  
   Data use supports organisational purpose and outcomes.

3. **Acquisition**  
   Data is acquired and generated for valid, transparent reasons.

4. **Performance**  
   Data is fit for purpose, reliable, and supports decision-making.

5. **Conformance**  
   Data practices comply with legislation, regulation, and policy.

6. **Human Behaviour**  
   Data use respects human rights, ethics, and social expectations.

**Key risk:**  
These principles are **intentionally high-level**. Treating them as operational requirements creates governance gaps.

---

## 6. Governance Requirements (What Organisations Must Put in Place)

While ISO/IEC 38505 avoids prescriptive controls, it **implicitly requires** organisations to be able to demonstrate:

- Clear **decision rights** relating to data
- Named **accountability** at executive and operational levels
- Visibility of **data risks**, including quality, misuse, and harm
- Mechanisms for **oversight and assurance**
- Alignment between **data practices and organisational values**

The standard assumes organisations already have:
- Legal and regulatory frameworks
- Supporting policies and procedures
- Management systems capable of reporting upward

---

## 7. Relationship to Other Frameworks and Standards

ISO/IEC 38505 is **foundational**, not sufficient on its own.

Common pairings include:
- **ISO/IEC 38500** - overall IT governance
- **DAMA-DMBOK** - data management practices
- **ISO/IEC 27001** - information security management
- **Health-specific standards and legislation** (e.g. patient safety, privacy, incident reporting)

Failure mode if used alone:
> Strong governance language with weak operational execution.

---

## 8. Practical Implications for Reporting and Analytics

In reporting and analytics contexts (including patient safety and incident reporting), ISO/IEC 38505 implies that governing bodies should be able to see:

- Which data assets are **authoritative**
- Who is accountable for **data quality and integrity**
- How data supports **clinical, safety, and system decisions**
- Where data-related risks are emerging
- Whether reporting aligns with **ethical and legal expectations**

It does **not** specify metrics, dashboards, or systems.

---

## 9. Limitations and Cautions

- The standard **cannot certify governance quality**
- Alignment is **contextual**, not absolute
- “ISO-aligned” is a **claim**, not a guarantee
- Evidence of governance must come from **practice**, not documentation alone

---

## 10. Summary Positioning Statement

ISO/IEC 38505 should be understood as:

> A **governance lens** for data — not a management manual, not a compliance checklist, and not a technical standard.

Its value lies in:
- Improving executive oversight
- Clarifying accountability
- Framing risk-aware conversations about data use
