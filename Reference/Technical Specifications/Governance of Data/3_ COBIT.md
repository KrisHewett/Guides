# COBIT (Data & Information Governance Components)

---

## 1. Overview

**COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies)** is a **governance and management framework** developed by **ISACA**. Its primary purpose is to help organisations **govern and manage information and technology** in a way that balances:

- Value creation
- Risk management
- Resource optimisation
- Assurance and accountability

Within COBIT, **data and information** are treated as **enterprise assets** that must be governed with the same rigour as finance, people, or infrastructure.

---

## 2. What COBIT Is (in Relation to Data)

In the context of data and information governance, COBIT provides:

- A **control‑oriented governance model**
- Explicit **objectives, practices, and activities**
- Clear separation between **governance** and **management**
- A structure suitable for **assurance, audit, and oversight**

COBIT answers the question:
> “How do we ensure information is governed, controlled, and used appropriately at an enterprise level?”

---

## 3. What COBIT Is Not

To avoid misapplication, COBIT is:

- **Not** a data management framework (e.g. DAMA‑DMBOK)
- **Not** a technical architecture or design method
- **Not** prescriptive about tools or platforms
- **Not** specific to any one sector or regulation
- **Not** sufficient on its own for operational data practice

COBIT focuses on **control and governance**, not day‑to‑day execution detail.

---

## 4. COBIT’s Governance vs Management Distinction

A defining feature of COBIT is its strict separation between:

### Governance
- Setting direction
- Evaluating needs and options
- Monitoring outcomes and compliance

### Management
- Planning and executing activities
- Building and operating systems
- Delivering services and controls

This distinction is critical for data governance, as it prevents:
- Executives designing controls
- Operational teams setting risk appetite

---

## 5. Where Data & Information Governance Sits in COBIT

COBIT does not isolate “data governance” as a single function. Instead, data and information governance is **embedded across multiple governance and management objectives**.

Key areas include:

### 5.1 EDM – Evaluate, Direct and Monitor (Governance Domain)

- **EDM01 – Ensure Governance Framework Setting and Maintenance**  
  Establishes enterprise‑wide governance, including information governance.

- **EDM03 – Ensure Risk Optimisation**  
  Covers data‑related risks such as quality failure, misuse, loss, or harm.

- **EDM04 – Ensure Resource Optimisation**  
  Treats data and information as resources requiring stewardship and prioritisation.

---

### 5.2 APO – Align, Plan and Organise (Management Domain)

- **APO01 – Managed I&T Management Framework**  
  Embeds data governance within management systems.

- **APO02 – Managed Strategy**  
  Aligns information and data use with enterprise strategy.

- **APO03 – Managed Enterprise Architecture**  
  Includes information and data architecture.

- **APO14 – Managed Data**  
  The most explicit data‑focused objective, covering:
  - Data ownership
  - Data lifecycle
  - Data quality
  - Metadata
  - Data classification and protection

---

### 5.3 DSS – Deliver, Service and Support

- **DSS01 / DSS06**  
  Operational handling of information, including controls and incident response.

---

### 5.4 MEA – Monitor, Evaluate and Assess

- **MEA01 – Performance and Conformance Monitoring**  
  Assurance that data governance controls are operating as intended.

---

## 6. Data & Information Governance Focus Areas in COBIT

Across these objectives, COBIT expects organisations to demonstrate governance over:

- **Data ownership and accountability**
- **Information quality and reliability**
- **Data security and protection**
- **Lifecycle management (creation to disposal)**
- **Compliance with legal and policy obligations**
- **Transparency and traceability for assurance**

These are framed as **control expectations**, not implementation instructions.

---

## 7. Intended Organisational Use

Appropriate uses of COBIT for data and information governance include:

- Structuring **enterprise governance and assurance models**
- Supporting **internal audit and risk functions**
- Clarifying **accountability and decision rights**
- Demonstrating governance coverage to executives and regulators
- Assessing whether controls exist and are monitored

Inappropriate uses include:
- Treating COBIT as a data management manual
- Implementing all objectives without prioritisation
- Assuming control existence equals effective data use

---

## 8. Relationship to Other Frameworks

COBIT is strongest when combined with complementary frameworks:

- **ISO/IEC 38505** – Governance principles and executive oversight  
- **DAMA‑DMBOK** – Data management capabilities and practices  
- **ISO/IEC 27001** – Information security management systems  
- **Sector legislation and policy** – Mandatory reporting and obligations

A common and effective pattern:
- **ISO 38505** → governance principles  
- **COBIT** → governance and control structure  
- **DMBOK** → operational data management practices  

---

## 9. Practical Implications for Reporting and Analytics

In reporting‑heavy environments (e.g. safety, performance, regulatory reporting), COBIT implies that an organisation should be able to demonstrate:

- Clear accountability for data used in reporting
- Controls ensuring data integrity and consistency
- Defined processes for managing data risk
- Monitoring and assurance over reporting outputs
- Escalation and response when controls fail

COBIT does **not** define:
- What indicators to report
- How analytics models should be built
- Which tools must be used
- What constitutes “good enough” data quality

---

## 10. Limitations and Risks

**Limitations**
- Control‑focused rather than outcome‑focused
- Can be heavy‑weight if applied indiscriminately
- Limited guidance on cultural and behavioural change

**Common risks**
- Over‑control that slows decision‑making
- Checklist compliance without real insight
- Audit satisfaction without user trust
- Confusing governance coverage with data usefulness

---

## 11. Positioning Statement

COBIT should be understood as:

> A **governance and control framework for information**, not a guide to managing data day to day.

Its strength lies in **assurance, accountability, and oversight**, not operational detail.
